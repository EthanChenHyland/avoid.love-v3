# CENTRAL IDEA

The central premise is:

> `avoid.love` is a system designed to help you avoid falling in love.

But there is a problem.

The visitor is already too late.

The website begins by treating attachment like something measurable, preventable, and correctable.

As the experience progresses, it discovers more and more evidence of the visitor's feelings.

Eventually the site reconstructs an entire implied love story out of:

- memories
- messages
- keepsakes
- habits
- details
- hesitation
- unsent thoughts
- attempts to pull away

The system tries harder and harder to neutralize those feelings.

It fails.

At the end, it stops trying.

# THE VISITOR IS THE PROTAGONIST

Do not make this primarily a story about fictional named characters.

The love story should belong to:

`YOU`

`THEM`

The user should project their own person and memories into the experience.

The site should talk directly to the visitor.

The emotional trick is:

the details are universal enough to apply broadly,

but specific enough to feel personal.

The visitor should think:

`why is this actually accurate?`

# THE LOVE STORY

V3 needs a REAL STORY ARC.

Not simply:

Screening
→ cool animation
→ another section
→ finale.

There should be an implied chronology of falling for someone.

Something approximately like:

### THE BEGINNING

They were initially just another person.

Then something small changed.

Maybe:

- a conversation lasted longer than expected
- they remembered something you said
- you started looking for them in a room
- one message became several
- you started recognizing their routines

### THE SMALL THINGS

Then the tiny details became important.

You remembered:

- how they take their coffee
- what song they recommended
- something they said casually
- when they usually wake up
- a joke no one else would understand
- a place you went together
- a date on the calendar

### THE ATTACHMENT

You started:

- rereading messages
- waiting for replies
- interpreting punctuation
- saving things
- noticing absence
- writing messages and deleting them
- thinking about them when something reminded you of them

### THE AVOIDANCE

Then you realized:

`oh no.`

So you tried to act normal.

Wait before replying.

Talk less.

Delete drafts.

Distract yourself.

Stop looking.

Stop checking.

Stop remembering.

### THE FAILURE

None of this works.

The evidence keeps accumulating.

And by the end:

you are still thinking about them.

That is the underlying love story.

# CHAPTER 06 — CONTAINMENT / ANTI-LOVE PROTOCOL

This is the system's final attempt.

One of V3's largest set pieces.

# PROTOCOL CLIMAX

The system should visibly succeed for a moment.

Everything disappears.

Silence.

Then:

A petal.

A message cursor.

# CHAPTER 07 — VOID / FAILURE

After the most visually dense scene:

almost nothing.

This contrast matters enormously.

Huge negative space.

Soft darkness or warm fog.

Very little UI.

Very little motion.

Maybe one object.

Possible copy:

`We tried.`

Pause.

`You could have left by now.`

Pause.

`But you're still here.`

Then:

`Oh.`

Do not fill this section with animation.

Stillness is part of the design.

# CHAPTER 08 — REVEAL / LOVE

The system gives up.

Not in defeat exactly.

More like recognition.

The clinical visual language disappears.

No more measurements.

No more labels.

No more redaction.

No more containment.

Earlier romantic objects return in a new form.

Possibilities:

- photo fragments come together
- dried flower opens/reappears
- red thread is no longer cut
- letter finally unfolds
- heart becomes warm instead of clinical
- objects that were archived drift freely
- rigid grid dissolves
- typography becomes organic
- lighting becomes intimate

Potential final copy:

`avoidance failed.`

or:

`still thinking about them?`

or:

`you can stop pretending in here.`

or simply:

`oh.`

Do not over-write the ending.

The visuals must carry it.
